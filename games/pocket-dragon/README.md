# Pocket Dragon: Glitchy's Quest

An original, family-friendly adventure for the platform's arcade, starring Glitchy, a mysterious baby dragon, in three levels that change
dimension as they go: a side view, a map from above, then the third dimension (a maze seen through Glitchy's eyes, and then the woods and
DeFu's lair in true 3D). **The whole quest** plays them as one run
against one clock: clear Egg Hills and its last portal shifts Glitchy into a new dimension, the Guild and the Castle; clear that and it
turns into the third dimension, the Whispering Woods. The hearts Glitchy has left carry from level to level, and losing them all (or
running out of a level's time) ends the quest. Win fast, with hearts to spare, for a place in the **Hall of Fame** (the ten best won quests,
by score and then time, saved with the progress). A level cleared in the quest (or alone) opens it to practise on its own with 1, 2 or 3;
only the whole quest reaches the Hall of Fame. It is client-only: no server, no network, nothing stored by the game itself (the platform
keeps progress and milestones). Nobody is ever hurt: a grumpy creature is cheered up, a beaten ghost dragon turns to sparkles, a guard
goes home dizzy, a bump costs a heart, and a tired guild takes a nap. The villain is DeFu, who wants every dragon egg for her rocket.

1. **Egg Hills** (a side-scrolling platformer). Run and jump across three stages: catch eggs and the golden egg on its high ledge, eat the
   fire berry, breathe fire to melt ice walls, and cheer up the gloomlings (land on them or puff fire at them). Each stage has its own
   weather: strong wind on the first (gusts, mostly against Glitchy, with calm between), heavy rain on the second (slippery grass, and
   fire puffs fizzle sooner) and deep fog on the third (only what is near Glitchy shows). Three hearts, a nest halfway that keeps your
   place, and a heart back after stages one and two.
   - **DeFu.** Each stage ends in her arena under her high ledge, and the portal stays sealed until her baby ghost dragons are
     beaten (three hits each, by landing on them or with fire). Before she strikes, her eyes glow red and the spots are marked on the
     ground; then her lasers come down there. She makes it harder each time: one ghost dragon and slow strikes on stage one; two ghost
     dragons, three security guards sent down one after another and strikes on two spots on stage two; and on stage three five guards,
     strikes on three spots and an egg rocket full of eggs. Six hits burst the rocket and set a dozen eggs free to catch.
   - **Secret charms.** One per stage, up cloud steps away from the path, showing itself only up close: a feather (stronger jumps), a
     shield (takes one bump for Glitchy) and a magnet (draws metaplasma from far off).
   - **Secret areas.** Every stage hides two, past its end behind walls and reached only by their doors: a burrow in the ground (it
     shows only up close; press down on it) digs into a cave tunnel of eggs and cave gloomlings whose ladder comes up at the nest, and a
     spring mushroom bounces Glitchy up into a swirl cloud and the sky islands, cloud steps of eggs with a rainbow down at the far end
     (falling off a cloud floats Glitchy down unhurt). The weather and DeFu's fight wait while Glitchy is in one.
   - **Power-ups and obstacles.** Wings (ten seconds of flapping: press jump in the air) and a forcefield bubble (ten seconds when
     nothing hurts, and a gloomling or guard bumped is cheered up) on every stage; steam vents that puff on and off, and from stage two
     boulders rolling to and fro (jump them, or land on top to bounce).
2. **The Guild and the Castle** (a small role-playing quest on a map seen from above, walked tile by tile anywhere on it). It has three
   parts, and finishing each one changes the whole land's look:
   - **Day: gather the guild.** Find what four dragon friends want so they join your guild and follow you, then use their powers (Bolt
     pushes boulders, Splash grows lily pads, Bloom parts brambles, Spark lights up a show). Bump a house's door to go in: the bakery,
     the library and the watchtower each hold a chest of gear (swift boots step faster, the cheer cape makes every show stronger, the
     ember helm breathes fire three tiles) and a clue on a stand.
   - **Night: save the eggs.** Once the guild is whole the sun sets. DeFu's security guards walk out with lanterns, carry the night's
     eight eggs off to their cage cart, and chase Glitchy when none is loose. Puff fire at a guard to leave it dizzy (it drops what it
     carries), walk onto a loose egg to save it, and open the cage to save every egg in it. A guard that bumps into Glitchy costs a heart;
     a forcefield bubble in the village keeps the guards off for ten seconds. Out of hearts, the level is lost.
   - **Winter: storm the castle.** With every egg safe, snow falls and the river freezes, so the land can be walked end to end. The gate
     opens to the three clues; inside, three grumble guards and the Grumble King stand between the guild and the **Legendary Golden
     Egg** on the throne.
   - **The guild levels up.** Everything the guild does is experience (friends, gear, clues, eggs, dizzy guards, keepers cheered,
     charms), shown as a level from one to ten: a higher level has more spirit in a challenge, and every four levels stronger shows.
   - **Cheer battles.** Each keeper is met on its own background (the courtyard, the torch hall, the starry tower and the throne room),
     with its grumble and the guild's spirit as bars. Each turn, put on a show (each keeper has a favourite, which counts twice, and a hint
     says which), a quick cheer (only one, but the keeper's grumble back is only one too) or evade (its next grumble misses). The same show
     twice running is too tired to work. The keeper grumbles back after each move; a guild out of spirit naps and tries again.
   - **Secret charms.** Three on the land, kept for the battles and used at once without taking a turn: spirit (back to full), a star (the
     next show counts three times) and a shield (blocks two grumbles).
3. **The Whispering Woods** (the third dimension). It has three parts, each in a scene of its own, with short scenes between them
   (the camera rising out of the maze, DeFu at her lair, the rocket defused or gone) that the action key skips:
   - **The maze** (seen through Glitchy's eyes). Read three clue stones and find the four parts of the egg launcher to open the moon gate;
     collect eggs for the launcher on the way, open five chests, light three lanterns with fire and melt the frozen hedge that hides the
     moon chest. Three secret charms hide in dead ends: swift feet (walk faster), the whole map (the map shows every path, and where the
     parts and eggs still are) and a metaplasma magnet.
   - **The woods** (seen from above, in 3D). Out of the maze the camera rises over the trees to DeFu's outpost. Free the three dragons
     in her cages (each follows Glitchy and brings a heart), open three chests for the rocket keys, take the forcefield bubble and the
     heart, and get past her four guards (a puff of fire leaves one dizzy; a second sends it home cheered up), or launch an egg at them
     from afar with the launcher. With the three keys, the lair's gate opens.
   - **The dungeon and castle** (DeFu's lair). DeFu stands on her platform beside her egg rocket, which leaves in two minutes. Her egg
     factory hatches shadow dragons that chase Glitchy (fire sets one free) and shields the rocket while it runs: break its three valves
     with fire, then fit the three keys into the rocket's panels to defuse it. Her laser glows along Glitchy's row (or column) before
     it burns, and her shockwave spreads out from her platform a tile at a time; the dungeon is out of reach of both. If the countdown
     runs out (or the hearts do), the rocket leaves for space and DeFu wins; defused, peace comes back to the woods.

The woods outside the maze are drawn in true 3D (three.js on WebGL 2): low-poly woods, a pond and DeFu's castle, jointed dragons that
walk, flap and swish their tails, guards, DeFu and her machines, moonlight with shadows, torches, and her laser and shockwave as light.
The camera follows Glitchy from above and behind and has its own path in each scene. The words and counts are drawn over it on the game
screen. Where there is no WebGL 2, the same woods are drawn flat from above, and the game plays the same.

**The backpack** goes with Glitchy everywhere, and in the quest from level to level. Berries, mushrooms and pineapples lie along the
way in every level (three in each Egg Hills stage, three on the castle's land, three in the maze and four out of it), and once Egg Hills'
fire berry is eaten the next fire berries go in the backpack too. Each is kept, nine at most of each, until the player uses it:

- **B eats a berry**: for twenty seconds fire reaches farther (a puff flies twice as far in Egg Hills and the maze, two tiles more in the
  castle, three tiles instead of one out of the maze). A berry also teaches fire, if the fire berry has not been found yet.
- **R rolls a mushroom's glitch blast**: a ball of glitch that rolls a long way and clears its path. In Egg Hills it rolls along the
  ground (up steps a tile high, down every drop) for about thirty tiles, cheering up gloomlings, glitching thorn bushes and boulders away,
  melting ice walls and sending DeFu's guards home; on the castle's land it rolls ten tiles through the night's guards (dizzy twice as long
  as from fire) and melts the ice around Bloom's seeds; in the maze it lights every lantern ahead and melts the frozen hedge; out of the
  maze it rolls ten tiles, sending guards home, setting shadow dragons free and breaking a valve of the egg factory. It stops at what
  cannot be walked through (and is never used indoors or in a cheer battle).
- **G eats a pineapple**: Glitchy grows twice as big, and the next bump only shrinks Glitchy back (it blinks a while) instead of costing a
  heart. A fall in Egg Hills still costs one.

The backpack shows at the right edge of the screen once anything is in it, with a flame bar while a blaze lasts and BIG while Glitchy is
big, and a line at the top says what was found or used.

**Metaplasma** is in every level: little bits of light that drift to Glitchy like pollen when it comes close. The page banks what every
run gathers ("Metaplasma banked"), to be turned into items one day, which will take a tremendous amount; for now it is only counted.

## Your card

Between runs, **Share card** makes a picture to post: a square (1:1), a story (9:16) or a wide post (16:9). At its heart is a trading card
of the game's own design: Glitchy's name and title, the run's score, a picture of the level with a stamp (cleared, try again, won it
all), two moves made from the run's counts, a quote of the game's own, and a foil frame by rarity: a spark for a run that fell short, a
flame for a cleared level, a blaze for winning the whole quest, and masterful once the masterful charm is earned. A quest's card shows its
time and the hearts kept as its two moves, and a won quest that makes the Hall of Fame shows its place there. Around it go a headline, the
trophy shelf (the milestones the run earned, each with a cup coloured by where it was won, or every trophy held on a card of the quest
so far), the three levels (cleared, open or locked, with their bests), the metaplasma banked, and an invitation. Before any run of a
visit, the card is of the quest so far.

The invite link is the platform's own page for the game. The game is never told the platform's address, so it reads it from the page
that frames it (its referrer) and only when that is the game's page (`/games/pocket-dragon`); anywhere else the card says to find the
game in the arcade instead. The platform's frame is `sandbox="allow-scripts"` with no `allow-downloads` and no `web-share`, so there
the Download and Share buttons are stopped by the browser and the clipboard may be refused; the panel says to save the picture from the
browser's own menu on the image (right-click, or press and hold), and the link is shown to select and copy. Letting the buttons work on
the platform is a change to the frame (`features/games/src/ui/game-player.tsx` and `packages/game-sdk/src/frame-headers.ts`) for its
owners to weigh. A won quest is saved with the progress, so a card can say so in a later visit.

## Test mode (for the admin)

The game's admin can play through everything without being stopped. Hold the game's title down for two seconds (mouse or finger)
between runs, type the admin key and press Unlock. A **Test mode** badge appears, and every level opens to choose. In a test run, a heart
lost is given back at once, DeFu's rocket countdown never runs out (it winds back by whole rounds of her weapons), and no level ends for
lack of time. Nothing is timed, scored, saved or sent: the page asks the platform for no run, records nothing, reports no milestone and
changes no progress or Hall of Fame. **Leave** on the badge ends it (and any test run being played); nothing remembers it after the visit.

The key is not in the game. `src/test-mode/key.ts` holds only a salted PBKDF2-SHA-256 hash of it (600,000 rounds), which can check a key
but not give it back, and the key is long and random. A key is read as its letters and digits, in any case. To change it, choose a new key
(at least 20 random letters and digits), and put the salt and hash this prints into `adminKey`:

```
node -e "const c=require('crypto');const k=process.argv[1].toUpperCase().replace(/[^0-9A-Z]/g,'');const s=c.randomBytes(16);console.log(s.toString('hex'),c.pbkdf2Sync(k,s,600000,32,'sha256').toString('hex'))" "YOUR-NEW-KEY"
```

The game is client-only, so someone who edits the page in their own browser could turn test mode on without the key. That is why test
mode can win nothing: its runs never reach the platform, and the verifier (which has no test mode) would earn nothing from them anyway.

## Controls

Arrows or WASD to go, an action key (space or Z) and a fire key (F or X). In Egg Hills left and right run and up or the action key jumps
(hold for a high jump, tap for a hop), down digs into a burrow, and with wings jump flaps in the air; in the woods up and down walk and
left and right turn in the maze, and outside it the arrows walk, fire breathes at the tile ahead and the action key frees a caged dragon,
opens a chest, fits a key or launches an egg (and skips a scene); in the castle the arrows
walk, the action key talks, picks up and opens (a door, a chest, the cage), and fire leaves a guard dizzy. In a cheer battle the arrows
move through the menu and the action key chooses, or the hotkeys act at once: 1 to 5 for the shows, Q a quick cheer, E evade and C a
charm. In every level B eats a berry, R rolls a mushroom's glitch blast and G eats a pineapple, from the backpack. Enter starts a run, P
pauses, and between runs 0 chooses the whole quest and 1, 2 and 3 a level open to practise. On a touch screen
there are buttons for the actions the level on the screen uses (in the quest they change with the level). M and N turn the music and the
effects off and on; the sound panel is at the bottom left. The card panel takes its own keys while it is open (Tab to move, Enter or Space
to press, Escape to close), so none of them start a run.

## How runs are checked

The rules know only ticks (sixty a second) and whole numbers: a pixel of Egg Hills is sixteen subunits, a cell of the woods is 256 and a
turn is 1,024 steps with a cosine table written out as integers, and the castle is a grid of tiles. Every level, maze, boss fight, hiding
place and keeper's favourite show is laid out from the seed alone; a run record holds only when a control was pressed or let go of, and
the rules play the ticks between. A record names what was played in its settings (`{ "cabinet": "hills" }`, or `"quest"`), and the one
published verifier (`src/rules/index.ts`) replays it with those rules. A quest can be half an hour long, so its presses are written
compactly to stay inside a record's 64 KB: one `keys` input holds up to sixty-four, each as the ticks since the one before and a letter
(`"12R30r0J"`: right pressed on tick 12, let go on tick 42, jump pressed on tick 42). The quest plays each level with that level's own
rules, so a level plays the same in the quest as alone; between levels the dimension shifts for three seconds, when nothing is taken,
and the next level starts with every control let go of. A whole quest won by the test bots is about 3 KB and replays in a tenth of a
second. The platform gives a run its seed and, on `run.submit`, replays the record; a
milestone the replay earns is held as replay-verified, which shows the run followed the rules, not that a person played it. The
verifier's SHA-256 is pinned in `configs/games/pocket-dragon.ts`; changing any level's rules changes the digest, and a test fails until
the pin and `verifierVersion` are updated together. The metaplasma banked is kept by the page with the rest of the progress; the rules
count what a run gathers, but the bank is not a milestone and is not checked.

## Milestones

Forty, all decided by the state of a run alone (`src/rules/*/milestones.ts`). Egg Hills has eleven of its own: eggs, golden eggs, fire,
ice melted, gloomlings cheered, the hills cleared, a clear without a bump, guards cheered up and the rocket's eggs freed. The woods have
ten, across their three parts: the maze's clues, chests, lanterns and moon chest, getting out of it (and within three minutes), the
launcher built; every caged dragon freed in the woods; and DeFu's factory broken and her rocket defused in the lair. The guild has eleven,
across day, night and winter: a first friend and the whole guild, every piece of gear, all eight of the night's eggs saved, the guild at
level five, the castle stormed, without a nap, a perfect pick, within four minutes, a charm used, and the **Masterful Pocket Dragon
Charm**, for storming the castle with every friend, every secret charm found, no nap, and every keeper won over with its favourite show
first. Two more can be earned in any level and are named once: a first secret charm, and 300 metaplasma in one run. The whole quest
earns every level's marks on the way (the same as each level played alone) and six of its own: winning it, winning without losing a
heart, winning with exactly one heart left, winning in ten minutes or less, winning with every egg (Egg Hills' three golden eggs, all
eight of the castle's night eggs saved and every egg of the woods' maze), and winning after visiting all six of Egg Hills' secret areas.
Some are earned on the same tick as a level's clear, but each asks for something more than the clear. The words are in the manifest.
The list is a draft for the owner to approve.

## Fairness

The tests hold what the generators guarantee: in Egg Hills the fire berry always comes at least six tiles before the first ice wall, pits
are at most two tiles wide, every stage has a golden egg on a ledge, a nest halfway, a secret charm with a ledge to reach it from and an
arena with nothing else in it, every run has at least 90 eggs, 15 blocks of ice and 8 gloomlings, and stages get more pits and faster
gloomlings as they go; in the woods every path cell can be reached, the parts, eggs and charms are each on their own cell that Glitchy can
walk to, the moon chest is a dead end behind the frozen hedge, and nothing shuts Glitchy in at the start. Players who see what is coming
(`test/support/*-bot.ts`) earn every milestone of each level on the seeds tried, and their records replay through the published verifier
to the same milestones: Egg Hills, all three of DeFu's fights included, without a bump; the woods with everything found, every caged
dragon freed and the rocket defused without a bump (dodging DeFu's laser and shockwave, whether the scenes are skipped or watched); the
castle with the Masterful Pocket Dragon Charm; and the whole quest, won in about four minutes with every heart
(`test/support/quest-bot.ts` plays each level with its own bot on the quest's clock). Two more quests earn the quest's last two marks:
one that takes every treasure of the woods wins with every egg, and one whose Egg Hills player also goes into each stage's tunnel and
sky (`test/support/hills-detours.ts`) wins having visited all six secret areas. A player who puts on the weakest shows and leaves
the charms behind still takes the golden egg, after a nap; the castle bot plays the night as it comes (the cage first, then the nearest
egg, then a puff of fire at any guard beside it or carrying an egg). A player who does nothing in DeFu's lair loses the rocket, and the
level still ends. Nobody has played it as a person yet.

## Tests

`pnpm --filter @game/pocket-dragon test:unit` (the rules, the stages, the quest, the screen and the page's parts), `test:integration`
(bundles the page and plays it against a simulated platform, including a quest on the platform's seed that is sent back and replayed),
`test:coverage`. The 3D view is tested without WebGL: its models, their animation, the camera's paths and the world set from a state
are plain three.js objects, and the renderer is stood in for; how it looks was checked in a browser.
